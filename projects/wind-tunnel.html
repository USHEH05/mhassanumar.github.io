Experimental Aerodynamics

Aerodynamic Wing Lift Analysis:
Effect of Wingspan on Lift Force

IB Extended Essay · Independent Research · Sohar, Oman

Year 2023
Type Independent Research
Datapoints 30,000
Wing Profiles 5 rectangular
01 — Research Question

Investigation

How does wingspan affect the lift force generated by a rectangular wing at the optimum angle of attack? This question was investigated to verify aspect ratio theory experimentally — specifically, whether higher aspect ratio wings produce measurably greater lift and better lift-to-drag ratios under controlled wind tunnel conditions.

02 — Methodology

Experimental Setup

The entire experimental apparatus was fabricated from scratch for this research. A wind tunnel was constructed with both a contraction cone (to accelerate and laminarise the airflow) and a diffuser cone (to decelerate flow after the test section and recover pressure), producing a controlled, uniform airstream in the test section.

Five rectangular wing profiles with varying wingspans but identical chord length were fabricated and tested sequentially. Each wing was positioned at its experimentally determined optimum angle of attack.

Data collection: A Vernier force sensor recorded lift and drag forces, logged continuously through LoggerPro. 30,000 datapoints were collected across all test runs, then processed to compute:

  • Lift coefficients (CL) for each wing
  • Induced drag values
  • Lift-to-drag (L/D) ratios
  • Aspect ratio correlation with measured lift

03 — Results

Findings

30K datapoints collected
5 wing profiles tested
CL lift coefficients computed per profile

The results confirmed aspect ratio theory: higher wingspan-to-chord ratio wings produced measurably greater lift coefficients and improved L/D ratios under identical conditions. The relationship followed the expected trend from thin airfoil and lifting line theory, with deviations attributed to tip vortex effects at lower aspect ratios.
